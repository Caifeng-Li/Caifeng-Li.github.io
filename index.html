Hello, I'm
Caifeng Li.

Exploring the genetic architecture of complex traits through statistical genetics, bioinformatics, computational biology, and interpretable machine learning.

My research focuses on the intersection of genetics, statistics and artificial intelligence. I study how genetic variation contributes to complex traits and diseases, especially when these relationships are nonlinear and difficult to capture with traditional statistical models. I develop statistical and machine-learning methods to simulate, analyze and interpret complex genetic patterns in biomedical data. My work aims to make genetic prediction more accurate, stable and biologically meaningful by integrating genetic variants, biological pathways and clinical information. Ultimately, I aim to help researchers better understand the genetic basis of complex diseases and support advances in disease prediction and personalized medicine.

01 / ABOUT

Research with a biological perspective.

I am a PhD student in Statistics at the University of Calgary, supervised by Dr. Quan Long and Dr. Jingjing Wu. My work lies at the intersection of statistical genetics, bioinformatics, systems biology, and machine learning.

I am especially interested in when additive models fail to capture complex biology, and how interpretable nonlinear approaches can reveal heterogeneity in disease mechanisms, subtypes, and treatment response.

Before embarking on my PhD journey, I earned a BEng in Bioengineering at Hebei University, followed by several years of professional experience in the medical diagnostics industry. I then completed an MSc in Computational Biology and Bioinformatics at Beijing Forestry University.
